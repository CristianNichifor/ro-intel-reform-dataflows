## Inspector General (IG)

- Appointed by Parliament in joint sitting for a fixed 5-year term; removal only by the same
  procedure
- **Unrestricted operational access:** no prior notice; direct access to premises, systems, files and
  personnel of all services
- Own budget line approved directly by Parliament, not routed through the services
- Reports audit findings to the JPC (existing flow T12) and publishes an unclassified annual report

## Internal audit unit

- Reports to the **IG, not to the service Director** — no dual-hatting, no loyalty conflict
- The audit plan is set by the IG; the Director may propose but not veto items
- Audit findings go to the IG, who decides what escalates to the JPC

## Whistleblower protection

- Implements EU Directive 2019/1937: internal channel to the IG, external channel to the Ombudsman
- Retaliation is prohibited with reversed burden of proof: any adverse measure within 12 months of a
  report is presumed retaliatory unless the service proves otherwise
- The IG may impose interim protective measures: transfer, anonymity, suspension of the alleged
  retaliator
- **Enforcement mechanism:** retaliation confirmed by the IG is a disciplinary offence and a ground
  for leadership removal (doc 14)

## Why internal control comes first (DCAF)

The DCAF Toolkit (2012) holds that internal control is the **prerequisite** for effective external
oversight: a service that cannot discipline itself cannot be overseen from outside. Internal control
generates the records, audit trails and culture of rule-compliance that parliamentary, judicial and
public oversight then consume. External oversight without internal control reduces to occasional
scandal response; internal control without external oversight decays into self-protection. The
layering of both is shown in `diagrams/oversight-layering.mmd`.
