# 19 · DCAF References

> DEMO // UNCLASSIFIED — SIMULATED DATA — NO REAL SYSTEMS

1. **DCAF — Security Sector Reform Backgrounder: Intelligence Oversight (2022).** Framing reference
   for why oversight must be layered (internal, executive, parliamentary, judicial, public).
2. **DCAF–NATO PA Report: Intelligence Oversight in the Euro-Atlantic Area (2026).** Comparative
   survey of oversight institutions; source of the convergence on parliamentary confirmation and
   fixed terms used in doc 14.
3. **DCAF — Overseeing Intelligence Services: A Toolkit (2012).** Authoritative operational guidance;
   basis of doc 18's internal-control-first principle and the IG's statutory powers.
4. **DCAF — Making Intelligence Accountable (Born & Leigh, 2005).** Classic taxonomy of
   accountability instruments; source of the "oversight visibility ≠ operational access" distinction
   used throughout this repo.
5. **U.S. DOJ — ODNI Post-Employment Violation Case (2026).** Enforcement precedent for the
   cooling-off regime (doc 16): declarations are inert without penalties.
6. **Rep. Jackson — FY2027 Intelligence Authorization Act Amendments (2026).** Legislative tightening
   of post-employment restrictions; model for statutory revision as evasion patterns emerge (doc 16).
7. **Bulgarian Parliamentary Appointment Reform Proposal (2025).** Model for public nomination and
   committee hearing before plenary votes on service heads (doc 14).
8. **Danish CSO Accountability Study (2026).** Source of the "agonistic accountability" concept in
   doc 17.
9. **Ukraine Intelligence Reform Analysis (2024).** Depoliticization and competitive selection of
   service leadership; mandate restriction as a governance tool (doc 14).
10. **Transparency International — Government Defence Integrity Index: Liberia (2026).** Independent
    vetting panels with published criteria as an anti-capture device (doc 14).
